Problem

A 2-page resume can't show who you are.

After years of shipping real products — $5.3M revenue from zero at Zenni, experimentation across 5 brands at Williams-Sonoma, mobile checkout rebuilt from scratch at Rodan + Fields — a resume still couldn't communicate how I think, how I work, or what I've actually built. I needed a portfolio. And I wanted to build it myself.

Motivation

Three goals. One project.

  • Create a portfolio that tells the full story of my work — not just bullet points
  • Prove I can go 0→1 with AI — not just talk about it, actually ship something real with it
  • Learn the full technical loop: design → code → deploy → iterate — end to end, myself

Approach

PM-directed AI development.

I didn't write code — I directed it. Every design decision, layout choice, color system, and section structure came from my own creative vision. Claude translated those decisions into working HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. I reviewed, iterated, and made every call on what shipped.

  • Designed every section from scratch — no templates, no UI kits, no inspiration boards
  • Used WisprFlow (voice-to-text) to direct Claude entirely by voice — didn't type a single prompt
  • Used ChatGPT to refine case study copy and sharpen how each story was framed
  • Used Gemini for personalized ideation — because it has my Google context, it could give suggestions grounded in my actual background, not generic answers
  • Iterated on mobile responsiveness, typography, and layout in real sessions — treating it like a real product sprint
  • Managed version control and deployment through GitHub Pages — commit, push, live

Budget Strategy

$20 budget. Real constraints. Real solutions.

Claude Pro has token limits per session. When I hit them mid-build, I didn't pause — I switched to VS Code Copilot and kept going. Two AI tools, zero downtime, one continuous project.

The constraint became the lesson: Managing a $20 budget across two AI tools to ship a production website is the same muscle as managing engineering capacity across two teams to ship a product. Prioritize, adapt, keep moving.

What I Learned

AI doesn't replace judgment. It amplifies it.

  • The quality of AI output is directly proportional to the clarity of your thinking — vague prompts produce vague results
  • PMs are actually well-positioned to direct AI development — we define requirements, review output, and make tradeoffs all day
  • Shipping a real product (even a personal one) requires the same discipline as shipping at work: mobile testing, edge cases, iterating on feedback
  • I can now build and launch a working website, tool, or prototype independently — that changes what I can do as a PM
